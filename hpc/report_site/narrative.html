    UMAP does not create the structure; it loses a little. For every encoder, trained or not, the full latent space scores at least as high as its 2-D map. Over the four focus Behavlets the trained encoders reach 0.36–0.38 before UMAP and 0.30–0.34 after, and cosine and Euclidean distance agree.

    The encoders roughly double what the raw input holds. Compared over the steps both sequences share (padding-aware), the raw sequences reach 0.17, and Masked UMAP keeps about that much (0.17). Plain UMAP on the padded input scores higher (0.22), but only because the padding hands it the sequence length: on the padded input, plain Euclidean distance recovers sequence length perfectly (1.00), and with it a good part of the ghost-kill count.

    Much of the encoders' advantage needs no training. In full dimension, the untrained MLP, VAE and Transformer already score 0.33–0.35, close to their trained versions. Only the LSTM depends on training (0.23 untrained, 0.38 trained). For the MLP and VAE the 2-D maps exaggerate what training adds, because UMAP loses more of the untrained space's structure (about 0.07) than of the trained one's (about 0.045).

    On the raw input, a linear 2-D reduction keeps even less. The padded input in full dimension reaches 0.30 over the four focus Behavlets with Euclidean distance, mostly through ghost kills (0.61). UMAP keeps 0.22 of that, PCA 0.10 and a random projection 0.06: both linear maps keep the sequence length (1.00) and lose almost everything else. So UMAP preserves more local structure than a linear reduction, but it is still the encoders, not UMAP, that lift the 2-D maps above the raw input.

    UMAP preserves what PCA loses, and five dimensions are almost enough. Switch the chart to 5-D: UMAP to five dimensions keeps nearly the full latent's score (LSTM 0.36 against 0.38), while PCA to five keeps 0.26–0.31 and to two only 0.09–0.14.

    The sections above look at one latent size at a time. This one puts every size on one chart, to ask whether the encoders beat the raw input only because they compress it. Each trained latent, from 32 to 512 dimensions, is scored unreduced and set against the raw input reduced to exactly the same width by UMAP, Masked UMAP, PCA or a random projection. Neighbours are Euclidean throughout, the same distance the pipeline uses on its reduced spaces, and the probe code reproduces the pipeline's own numbers on those to within 0.0001.

    Latent size hardly matters once nothing is reduced. Over the four focus Behavlets every trained encoder sits between 0.33 and 0.38 from 32 to 512 dimensions, the Transformer a little below the rest.

    No raw baseline reaches the encoders at any width. Masked UMAP, the padding-aware raw baseline, levels off at 0.21–0.23 from five dimensions up. Plain UMAP stays near 0.25, while PCA and the random projection climb towards the padded input's own 0.30 as their width grows. Those three only get there by keeping the sequence length perfectly (neighbourhood hit 1.00 on length): pick Sequence length to see it. The encoders keep much less of it (0.14–0.37).

    What training adds does not depend on latent size. Tick Untrained controls: at 64, 128 and 512 dimensions the untrained LSTM stays at 0.23, the MLP at 0.32 and the VAE at 0.34. Against the trained encoder of the same size that is a gap of 0.13–0.15 for the LSTM, about 0.06 for the MLP and 0.03 for the VAE. The untrained Transformer sits within 0.02 of the trained one at every size (0.34, 0.34 and 0.36), so its training adds nothing to the neighbourhoods at any width.

    Every neighbourhood hit above counts each sequence's five nearest neighbours. Here the neighbourhood grows to a thousand sequences, with the hit still adjusted so that 0 is chance (calculate_neighborhood_hit_profile). A label whose sequences form many small, tight islands scores high at small k and falls off fast; one that runs as a gradient, or forms a few large groups, holds up further.

    The structure is mostly local. In every space the score falls as the neighbourhood grows. Over the four focus Behavlets the trained latents go from 0.36–0.38 at five neighbours to 0.11–0.13 at a thousand. Ghost kills reach furthest: the LSTM latent still scores 0.53 at 200 neighbours and 0.25 at a thousand, while the two ghost-distance Behavlets are down to 0.07–0.10 by a hundred.

    Training mostly sharpens the nearest neighbours. Tick Untrained controls. The trained LSTM leads its untrained copy by 0.15 at five neighbours and by 0.02 at a thousand; the MLP's lead shrinks from 0.06 to 0.04. At a thousand neighbours the trained latents (0.11–0.13) are close to the untrained ones (0.09–0.10) and to the padding-aware raw input (0.10).

    The padding-aware raw input runs the other way. It scores 0.13 at five neighbours, rises to 0.20 at fifty and only then falls. Its nearest neighbours are often wrong: comparing two sequences only over the steps both have lets a short attack match the opening of a longer one, so the five nearest neighbours share sequence length less often than chance (−0.20) and agree on ghost kills only at 0.05. At fifty neighbours the kill count is back to 0.34. So the raw input does hold the coarse kill structure; what it lacks, and the encoders supply, is a local neighbourhood that respects it.

    UMAP builds larger groups than the latent it reduces. Switch to 2-D. The 2-D maps start below their full latents but fall more slowly, and somewhere between 50 and 200 neighbours they overtake them: 0.13–0.17 at a thousand against 0.11–0.13. The LSTM map keeps ghost kills at 0.65 over 500 neighbours, against 0.36 in the full latent. UMAP's 5-D maps behave the same. PCA in two dimensions stays flat and low (0.09–0.14 at five neighbours, 0.05–0.09 at a thousand), and the padded raw input, which ranks neighbours by length, falls fastest (0.30 to 0.06).

    Every measure so far is label-free: neighbours, distances and clusters never see a Behavlet, which is what lets them test whether an unsupervised space makes the constructs salient. A linear probe asks a different question. A ridge regression is fitted, with the labels, from a space to each Behavlet's value (calculate_probe_measures; out-of-fold R², five folds), so it finds the construct wherever it sits. It says whether the information was kept, not whether it was discovered. Read it against two references: the raw input at full width, and sequence length alone.

    The information is already in the input. From the raw sequences, with each one's last step held over its padding so that the padding gives nothing away, ridge reaches R² 0.68 over the four focus Behavlets (ghost kills 0.89). Sequence length alone reaches 0.01 (0.01 for each of the four). So the raw input's 0.68 is not the sequence length in disguise: holding the last step over the padding leaves a linear read-out nothing to take from the length, and the length on its own predicts none of the four. A one-column linear probe can only use a straight-line relation, so this rules out the simple leak, not every way the length could matter.

    Training adds a little, and not only training. The trained latents reach 0.78–0.82 at 512 dimensions, 0.71–0.74 at 32. The untrained MLP and VAE already reach 0.74–0.76 and 0.76–0.82 at the sizes they were run, within 0.03 of their trained versions, and the untrained Transformer 0.69–0.77. A randomly initialised network is a fixed non-linear feature map, and a linear model on enough random features reads a great deal. Only the untrained LSTM falls below the raw input (0.49–0.52).

    Present everywhere, salient only after training. Set beside the neighbourhood hit, this is the result. The untrained MLP and VAE carry the Behavlets as well as their trained versions do for a linear read-out, yet their neighbourhoods agree with them less (0.32 and 0.34, against about 0.38 for both trained versions). The raw input carries them at 0.68, yet its padding-aware neighbourhoods reach 0.13–0.17. Training does not put the constructs into the representation; it makes them the thing that decides which sequences are close.

    Reduction keeps neighbourhoods or directions, not both. The table compares each 512-dimensional latent with its own reductions. The LSTM's five principal components predict the four focus Behavlets with R² 0.56, its 5-D UMAP with 0.36 and its 2-D map with 0.24, although PCA's five components score lowest of the three on neighbourhood hit (0.31 against 0.36 and 0.34). UMAP keeps neighbourhoods and bends the directions a linear model reads; PCA keeps those directions.

    The trained latents also carry sequence length. Padding never reaches the training loss, yet ridge recovers sequence length from the trained LSTM's latent with R² 0.94 and from the VAE's with 0.83, against 0.35 and 0.68 untrained. The 2-D maps keep almost none of it linearly (0.03 or less).

    Sixty-three of the 263 participants played at least ten levels. For them, measures built on distances along the path agree closely between the two spaces: mean step length, step novelty, recurrence rate, laminarity and the longest vertical line all have ρ near 0.8. Cluster-based measures agree less (0.58–0.77), because HDBSCAN draws different borders in each space. Population novelty (0.40) and the unclustered share (0.35) depend on where each space happens to be dense, and are the least portable.

    No measure correlates with performance, flow or BIS/BAS once the 130 measure × variable tests are Bonferroni-corrected, in either space. Uncorrected, 18 pairs (LSTM) and 16 pairs (UMAP only) reach p < .05. That is more than the six or seven expected by chance, but the tests are not independent and the pairs differ between the two spaces. The strongest LSTM result, lower cluster entropy alongside a higher maximum score (r = −0.40, corrected p = .13), has no counterpart in the UMAP-only space.

    Part of what remains is how much a participant played. The number of levels correlates with maximum score (r = 0.42) and, inside the latent measures, with clusters visited (0.50–0.56), the longest vertical line (0.36–0.44) and cluster entropy (−0.35 to −0.37). A follow-up should partial it out before reading any of these as style. BIS/BAS scores exist for 56 of the 63 participants.

    The sweep. 84 configurations: four encoders (LSTM, MLP, VAE, Transformer) × five latent sizes (32–512) × two feature sets × two sequence types, plus UMAP with no encoder for each feature set and sequence type. Every encoder trained for 100 epochs with seed 42, global normalisation and per-level speed correction. Latent vectors were reduced to two dimensions with UMAP (cosine metric, 15 neighbours) and clustered with HDBSCAN (minimum cluster size 20). Three more no-encoder baselines were rebuilt locally from the UMAP-only configuration: Masked UMAP, PCA and a seeded Gaussian random projection. All four no-encoder baselines were also rebuilt at five dimensions and at every latent size. The h512 encoders were rebuilt with PCA in place of UMAP, and with both reduced to five dimensions, from their saved checkpoints without retraining.

    Validation. Behavlets and per-sequence metadata are binned into levels. Neighbourhood hit is the share of each sequence's five nearest neighbours in the 2-D layout that share its level, computed without sequences that have no instance of the Behavlet, then adjusted so that 0 is chance. The comparison table and line chart read the sweep's own numbers. The profile repeats the hit at 5 to 1,000 neighbours from one nearest-neighbour search per space (exact, also for the padding-aware raw distance). Ridge R² is out-of-fold over five shuffled folds, with the penalty chosen inside each training fold, near-constant columns dropped and standardised features clipped at ±10. The length-only reference fits the same probe from a single column, each sequence's length.

    Rebuilt spaces. The figures and all of RQ2 need per-sequence coordinates, which the sweep does not keep. Those spaces were rebuilt on this machine from the sweep's saved checkpoints, using the sweep's own configuration and code path and without retraining. The table below shows how closely each rebuild matches the sweep.

    Participant measures. Gaussian KDE with one Scott-rule bandwidth for the whole space. Step novelty is the negative log density of a level under a KDE fitted to the participant's earlier levels (after two levels of history), calibrated across participants with a quantile transform. Population novelty uses a KDE fitted to all sequences. Recurrence quantification uses a fixed radius of 10% of the space's largest pairwise distance and Euclidean distance. Only participants with at least ten levels are summarised.

First-five-second sequences all have the same length, so the plain baseline is fair there.
