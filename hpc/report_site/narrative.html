    • Four Behavlets carry the attack-phase constructs: ghost kills, chasing ghosts rather than collecting dots, and the average distance kept from ghosts outside and during the power-pill phase.

    With the five Experimental2 features (four ghost distances and the score), every trained encoder groups sequences by these Behavlets better than UMAP alone. The LSTM leads on ghost kills, 0.86 at latent size 512 against 0.50 for UMAP only, and it gains the most from a larger latent space. The untrained rows show where that comes from. The untrained LSTM lands near UMAP only, so its gain comes from training. The untrained MLP and VAE already sit well above UMAP only, so part of their score is the architecture. The untrained Transformer scores as high as the trained one, so on these Behavlets its training adds nothing. On the on-pill ghost distance, the untrained MLP, VAE and Transformer even score slightly above their trained versions. With all 267 features, every model sits close to chance on all four, so the extra columns bury the attack behaviour rather than add to it.

    Each panel colours one two-dimensional layout by one Behavlet. Pick an encoder to see its trained space above the same encoder with its initial weights. In the trained LSTM, ghost kills occupy their own regions, from one kill in the middle to three or four at the bottom left, and the chase-or-collect Behavlet follows the same layout. The trained MLP, VAE and Transformer form one continuous layout with kill counts running as a gradient from none to three or four.

    The untrained encoders already split kill counts into separate islands, because kills show up as score jumps in their input, but they mix counts more within each island. UMAP only forms islands by sequence length and mixes kill counts inside them.

    Every score above is measured after UMAP has squeezed each space into two dimensions, so on its own it cannot separate the encoder's work from UMAP's. Here the same neighbourhood hit is measured on both sides of UMAP: in each encoder's full 512-dimensional latent space, and in the raw input before any embedding.

    UMAP does not create the structure; it loses a little. For every encoder, trained or not, the full latent space scores at least as high as its 2-D map. Over the four focus Behavlets the trained encoders reach 0.36–0.38 before UMAP and 0.30–0.34 after, and cosine and Euclidean distance agree.

    The encoders roughly double what the raw input holds. Compared over the steps both sequences share (padding-aware), the raw sequences reach 0.18, and Masked UMAP keeps about that much (0.17). Plain UMAP on the padded input scores higher (0.22), but only because the padding hands it the sequence length: on the padded input, plain Euclidean distance recovers sequence length perfectly (1.00), and with it a good part of the ghost-kill count.

    Much of the encoders' advantage needs no training. In full dimension, the untrained MLP, VAE and Transformer already score 0.33–0.35, close to their trained versions. Only the LSTM depends on training (0.23 untrained, 0.38 trained). For the MLP and VAE the 2-D maps exaggerate what training adds, because UMAP loses more of the untrained space's structure (about 0.07) than of the trained one's (about 0.045).

    A linear read-out tells the same story. A ridge regression predicts the four focus Behavlets' values from the trained latents with out-of-fold R² 0.78–0.83, and from the raw sequences with 0.68. The input already carries these quantities almost linearly, which is the circularity noted under the caveats. From the 2-D maps R² drops to 0.24–0.37, as expected from two coordinates.

    The trained latents also carry sequence length, despite the masking. Padding never reaches the training loss, yet the trained LSTM's latent predicts sequence length with R² 0.94 and the VAE's with 0.84, against 0.35 and 0.68 untrained. The 2-D maps keep almost none of it linearly (R² 0.03 or less), and their neighbourhoods keep less than the full latents do (0.07–0.18 against 0.15–0.31).

    Sixty-three of the 263 participants played at least ten levels. For them, measures built on distances along the path agree closely between the two spaces: mean step length, step novelty, recurrence rate, laminarity and the longest vertical line all have ρ near 0.8. Cluster-based measures agree less (0.58–0.77), because HDBSCAN draws different borders in each space. Population novelty (0.40) and the unclustered share (0.35) depend on where each space happens to be dense, and are the least portable.

    No measure correlates with performance, flow or BIS/BAS once the 130 measure × variable tests are Bonferroni-corrected, in either space. Uncorrected, 18 pairs (LSTM) and 16 pairs (UMAP only) reach p < .05. That is more than the six or seven expected by chance, but the tests are not independent and the pairs differ between the two spaces. The strongest LSTM result, lower cluster entropy alongside a higher maximum score (r = −0.40, corrected p = .13), has no counterpart in the UMAP-only space.

    Part of what remains is how much a participant played. The number of levels correlates with maximum score (r = 0.42) and, inside the latent measures, with clusters visited (0.50–0.56), the longest vertical line (0.36–0.44) and cluster entropy (−0.35 to −0.37). A follow-up should partial it out before reading any of these as style. BIS/BAS scores exist for 56 of the 63 participants.

    The sweep. 84 configurations: four encoders (LSTM, MLP, VAE, Transformer) × five latent sizes (32–512) × two feature sets × two sequence types, plus UMAP with no encoder for each feature set and sequence type. Every encoder trained for 100 epochs with seed 42, global normalisation and per-level speed correction. Latent vectors were reduced to two dimensions with UMAP (cosine metric, 15 neighbours) and clustered with HDBSCAN (minimum cluster size 20).

    Validation. Behavlets and per-sequence metadata are binned into levels. Neighbourhood hit is the share of each sequence's five nearest neighbours in the 2-D layout that share its level, computed without sequences that have no instance of the Behavlet, then adjusted so that 0 is chance. The comparison table and line chart read the sweep's own numbers.

    Rebuilt spaces. The figures and all of RQ2 need per-sequence coordinates, which the sweep does not keep. Those spaces were rebuilt on this machine from the sweep's saved checkpoints, using the sweep's own configuration and code path and without retraining. The table below shows how closely each rebuild matches the sweep.

    Participant measures. Gaussian KDE with one Scott-rule bandwidth for the whole space. Step novelty is the negative log density of a level under a KDE fitted to the participant's earlier levels (after two levels of history), calibrated across participants with a quantile transform. Population novelty uses a KDE fitted to all sequences. Recurrence quantification uses a fixed radius of 10% of the space's largest pairwise distance and Euclidean distance. Only participants with at least ten levels are summarised.

  • One seed per configuration. Differences of about 0.01 between latent sizes or models are inside run-to-run variation that this sweep does not measure. Rank models only on gaps well above that.
  • The Experimental2 features are what the focus Behavlets are computed from. Caution2 is literally the average ghost distance, and ghost kills appear as jumps in the score. The encoders never saw a label, so the agreement is real, but part of it reflects the input already carrying these quantities. The untrained encoders show how much: on ghost kills they reach 0.53 to 0.66 before any training.
  • Untrained controls exist for latent size 512 on Experimental2 only. They and the trained spaces they are compared with in the text come from local rebuilds, so they match each other; the comparison table's trained rows are the sweep's own numbers.
  • UMAP only sees sequence length on attack sequences. Without an encoder, the flattened input keeps the padding, so the layout follows how long each attack lasted. That is why it scores almost perfectly on level and sequence length, and why those sets are left out of the comparison. Masked UMAP compares sequences only over the steps both have and is the fairer raw baseline; it was rebuilt locally, since the sweep never ran it. First-five-second sequences all have the same length, so the plain baseline is fair there.
  • Full-dimension and 2-D neighbourhoods are not the same geometry. Five nearest neighbours among 512 dimensions and among two are found under different conditions, so the probes compare the direction of each change more safely than its size. All probes come from local rebuilds.
  • Rebuilt spaces are close to the sweep, not identical. Adjusted neighbourhood hit differs by at most 0.015, but HDBSCAN finds a different number of clusters. UMAP only differs as well, so the cause sits upstream of the encoder: numerical differences between the cluster and this machine, or differences in data or code between them.
  • Participant analyses are exploratory. They cover 63 participants, use Pearson correlations, and do not yet adjust for the number of levels played.
